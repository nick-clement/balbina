Adolescence is a time of big change. Identity is forming, friendships shift, school demands rise and online life adds its own pressure. For some teenagers, worry builds until it takes over: racing thoughts, avoiding school or social events, panic, irritability or withdrawal.

## What you might notice

A teenager may seem on edge, snappy or flat. They may struggle to sleep, complain of aches, avoid things they used to enjoy, or spend more time alone. Often they cannot say what is wrong, and may not know themselves.

## How therapy helps

Therapy offers a private, confidential space to make sense of what is happening. My approach with teenagers is psychoanalytic, which means exploring what lies beneath the worry rather than only managing symptoms. Over time, young people tend to gain insight, resilience and a stronger sense of themselves.

## How parents can support

Stay available without pressing for answers. Talk side by side, such as in the car or on a walk. Our journal has some ideas on [talking to a teenager who has gone quiet](/journal/talking-to-a-quiet-teenager).

If you are concerned about your teenager's safety, speak to your GP, and call 999 if they are in immediate danger.
