Children and teenagers feel low for many reasons: loss, change, friendship difficulties, pressure, or feelings they cannot name. Sometimes sadness looks like irritability, tiredness, or not wanting to do much at all.

## What you might notice

A child may seem flat, tearful or withdrawn. They may lose interest in play or hobbies, sleep more or less than usual, or say they are not good enough. Younger children may complain of aches, or become clingy.

## How therapy helps

Therapy offers a safe, confidential space where feelings can be spoken, drawn or played out without judgement. We explore what sits beneath the low mood and help a young person find their own words and strengths. My approach is attachment focused and trauma informed, and psychoanalytic with teenagers.

## What parents can do

Keep routines steady, spend unpressured time together and listen more than you advise. Let your child know that all feelings are allowed.

If low mood lasts, or you are worried about your child's safety, speak to your GP. Call 999 in an emergency.
