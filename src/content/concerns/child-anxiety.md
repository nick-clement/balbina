Every child worries. Anxiety becomes a concern when the worry is large, lasts, and starts to shrink a child's world. A child may cling at the school gate, need constant reassurance, avoid new places, or lie awake at night. Some show it through temper or tears rather than words.

## What is often underneath

Anxiety is not a flaw in a child. It is a signal that something feels unsafe or too big to hold. It may be linked to change, loss, a hard experience, or simply a sensitive temperament. Understanding what the worry is about matters more than talking a child out of it.

## How therapy helps

In sessions, your child has a private, steady space to explore their thoughts and feelings at their own pace, often through play and drawing. My approach is attachment focused and trauma informed. Over time, children usually find more room to feel brave, and parents gain a clearer picture of what their child needs.

## What parents can do now

Stay calm and close. Name the feeling without feeding it ("That sounds scary"), and keep to routines that help your child feel safe. Our journal has [more on bedtime worries](/journal/bedtime-worries-and-sleep) and [school worries](/journal/worries-about-school).

If your child's anxiety is affecting daily life, a conversation can help you decide the next step. The first call is free.
