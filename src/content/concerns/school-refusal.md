School refusal, sometimes called emotionally based school avoidance, describes children who find it very hard or impossible to attend. Mornings may bring tears, stomach aches, anger or silence. Some children cope at school and fall apart at home. Others stop going altogether.

## What lies behind it

The causes vary. Anxiety is common, and so are difficulties with friendships, learning, sensory overload, a change at home or a loss. Sometimes a child cannot say why. The behaviour is a signal, and the work is to understand it.

## How therapy helps

Your child gets a steady, private space to explore what school stirs up. Alongside this, parent sessions help you respond with confidence, reduce the daily battle and think about a gradual return. My approach is attachment focused and trauma informed.

## What you can try now

Keep mornings calm and predictable. Listen without trying to fix. Tell the school what you are seeing. Our journal has more on [when school feels too hard](/journal/worries-about-school).

Please speak to your GP if your child's worry is affecting their health or if attendance is falling.
