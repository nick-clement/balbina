Shouting, hitting, refusing, slamming doors. When a child's behaviour is hard to live with, it can leave a family exhausted. Under the anger there is usually something else: fear, hurt, embarrassment, a feeling of being out of control.

## Seeing behaviour as communication

Children often cannot tell us what is wrong, so they show us. Behaviour that seems out of proportion may be telling you about a worry, a loss, something that happened at school, or a need that is not being met. Punishment alone seldom reaches that.

## How therapy helps

In sessions, your child can express feelings through play and talk in a space where they are not judged. Parent sessions help you understand what your child is communicating and how to respond, which eases stress at home. My approach is attachment focused and trauma informed.

## What you can try now

Stay steady, set clear limits without shouting, and talk once everyone is calm. Our journal has more on [anger in children](/journal/anger-in-children) and [big feelings and meltdowns](/journal/big-feelings-and-meltdowns).

If behaviour is putting anyone at risk, please get urgent help from your GP or call 999.
