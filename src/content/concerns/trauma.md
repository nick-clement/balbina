Children can be affected by experiences that frightened, hurt or overwhelmed them. These might be a single event or something ongoing. The effects often show in behaviour: sleep trouble, clinginess, anger, being easily startled, difficulty concentrating, or going quiet.

## Why safety comes first

A child who has been through something frightening needs to feel safe before they can begin to make sense of it. Work is gentle and paced by the child. Nobody is pushed to talk about what happened.

## How therapy helps

A consistent, caring relationship gives a child the experience of being understood, which is healing in itself. Through play and talk, they can slowly find ways to express what they carry. My approach is attachment focused and trauma informed. Parents are supported so that the care at home and in therapy fit together.

## For parents

You may be worried, or carrying your own distress. Support for you is part of helping your child. Early understanding makes a difference.

If a child is in immediate danger, call 999. If you have safeguarding concerns, contact your local children's services or the NSPCC.
