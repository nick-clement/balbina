Friendships matter enormously to children and teenagers. Falling out, being left out, struggling to make friends or finding closeness frightening can be painful and lonely. For some young people, the same difficulty repeats in each new friendship or relationship.

## What it can look like

A young person may seem withdrawn, anxious about school or social events, or quick to feel rejected. They may cling to one friend, push people away, or feel they do not belong anywhere.

## How therapy helps

Therapy is itself a relationship, and a safe one in which to notice patterns. Over time, young people often come to understand what happens for them in closeness, trust and conflict, and find more freedom in how they relate. My approach with teenagers and young adults is psychoanalytic, which means looking at what lies beneath.

## For parents

Listen without rushing to fix. Let your child know that belonging takes time and that they are not alone. If bullying is involved, tell the school and keep a record of what happens.

If your child's distress is lasting, talking it through can help you decide the next step.
