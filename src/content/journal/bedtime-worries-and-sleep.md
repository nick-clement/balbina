When the house goes quiet, worries get louder. Many children find bedtime the hardest part of the day because there is nothing left to distract them from what is on their mind.

## Build a steady wind-down

Follow the same short sequence each night: bath, pyjamas, a story, lights out. Predictability tells the body it is safe to rest.

## Make room for worries earlier

Set aside a few minutes before the bedtime routine begins to talk about the day. A worry notebook can help older children get things out of their head and onto paper.

## Respond with warmth and consistency

If your child comes out of their room, take them back calmly and kindly each time. Short, quiet and boring works better than long negotiations.

## When to seek help

If sleep problems last for weeks, come with nightmares or strong fears, or follow a difficult event, it is worth asking for support. Your GP is a good place to start.
