Anger is often the part of a feeling that shows. Underneath it there can be fear, disappointment, embarrassment or sadness that a child has no words for yet.

## Stay steady

Children borrow our calm. Keep your voice low and your body relaxed, and set clear limits without shouting. "I will not let you hit. You can stamp your feet or squeeze this cushion."

## Talk once things have cooled

Wait until everyone is calm, then wonder out loud together: "I think you were really upset when your friend said no." Guessing at the feeling often helps a child feel understood.

## Offer ways to let it out

Drawing, running, pillow-punching and rough play are safe outlets. Teaching a child to notice the first signs in their body, such as a hot face or tight fists, helps them catch anger earlier.

## When to ask for help

If anger leads to harm, happens most days, or is damaging friendships and family life, it is time to talk to a professional.
