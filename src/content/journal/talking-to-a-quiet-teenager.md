Pulling away from parents is a normal part of growing up. Teenagers are working out who they are, and that often means more time alone and fewer words at the dinner table.

## Go where the talking happens

Many teenagers talk more side by side than face to face. Car journeys, walks and washing up can open a conversation that a sit-down chat never will.

## Listen more than you fix

When your teenager does share something, resist the urge to solve it straight away. "That sounds really hard" can matter more than advice.

## Keep the door open

Stay available without pushing. A simple "I am here whenever you want to talk" lets them know the offer stands.

## Know the signs that need attention

Withdrawal that lasts, big changes in sleep or eating, loss of interest in things they used to enjoy, or any talk of hurting themselves are reasons to seek help. Speak to your GP, and if you believe your child is in immediate danger, call 999.
