A meltdown is not a child choosing to behave badly. It is a child whose feelings have become bigger than their ability to manage them. Young children are still building the parts of the brain that calm things down, so they often need an adult to lend them that calm.

## In the moment

Keep your voice low and your words few. Get down to their level and stay close. Reasoning, lectures and consequences can wait, because a child in the middle of a storm cannot take them in.

## Afterwards

When things are quiet, name what happened in simple words: "You were so cross when the game had to stop." Children learn to understand their feelings when someone puts words to them.

## Look for patterns

Notice when meltdowns happen. Tiredness, hunger, transitions and busy days are common triggers. If the outbursts are frequent, very intense, or getting worse, it is worth talking to someone who can help you work out what lies underneath.
